# 144. A browser shares one stream, and its tabs are Watchers by beats

2026-11-02, relates to 55, 58, 73, 87

**Context:** every board tab and index tab held its own EventSource (`/api/board/:id/events`, `/api/events`). Chrome allows six connections per host over plain HTTP, and the PM reaches the board through an SSH tunnel, so jumping fast between boards left the seventh page waiting for a free slot. A Watcher was its connection: the stream closing was how the daemon learned a tab had gone, which the stranded rule's banner after the grace rests on. **Decision:** all tabs of one browser share one stream, `GET /api/stream`, held by a SharedWorker. The worker registers each board tab (`POST /api/stream/:id/watch`) and the daemon subscribes it as a Watcher on its board with the shared connection as its socket, so the stranded rule, the auto-open check and `clients` read it unchanged. Presence comes from beats: a tab holds a Web Lock for its life and beats the worker every 5 s; the worker drops a tab on the lock freeing, on `pagehide` or after 75 s silent, and beats the daemon every 5 s for the tabs it holds; the daemon drops a Watcher unnamed for 20 s and every Watcher of a stream that closes. Rejected: dropping the stream in hidden tabs, which makes a tab behind the terminal read as nobody watching and fires false stranded banners; HTTP/2, which multiplexes the streams but needs a trusted local certificate on both Macs; tab beats alone, since Chrome runs a tab's timers once a minute after five minutes hidden, so a close would take over a minute to count. **Consequences:** a closed tab still drops its Watcher at once (the lock), a crashed worker at once (its stream closes), a hung one within 20 s. The worker's URL is named by its bytes, so a daemon update hands new tabs a new worker while open tabs keep theirs; `/api/board/:id/events` and `/api/events` stay for the menu bar, for a browser with no SharedWorker and for a tab still on an older page.
