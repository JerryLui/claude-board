# 86. The status item learns by push and survives by poll

2026-08-10 · replaces 79; relates to 77; relates to 72

**Context:** ADR 79 had the status item poll at the widget's fifteen-second cadence precisely to avoid opening an SSE connection from the Objective-C client, and priced that as up to one tick of disagreement after a control press on the other surface. In practice a pomodoro setting saved on the index page took up to seventeen seconds to reach the menu bar — long enough to look broken, and the daemon's only stream was board-scoped, so there was nothing a board-less process could have joined anyway. **Decision:** the daemon publishes timer, settings and waiting-count changes on a daemon-wide stream (`GET /api/events`), and the menu bar holds that stream open beside the untouched fifteen-second poll. Every push funnels into the same fetch the poll runs (`cb_poll_once`), so state application has one owner and freshness advances only on a real fetch, never on stream liveness. The stream reconnects with bounded exponential backoff; the poll is the fallback. **Consequences:** the two surfaces agree within a second in either direction, and a stream that drops, never opens, or outlives a daemon restart degrades to exactly the behaviour ADR 79 described rather than to a frozen icon. The price is the SSE client in Objective-C that 79 existed to avoid, and a reconnect path that is only trusted because the checks kill and restart real daemons rather than reasoning about the code.
